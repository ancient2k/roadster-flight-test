# Level editor (design)

A track editor in the spirit of the classic DOS racing games of 1990: a 2D tile grid seen from above, a palette of
track pieces, and a "test drive" that drops the car into the 3D world at once. Sections marked *planned* are not
built yet.

Keys in the editor: click to place, right click to erase, R rotate, E level (elevated road), wheel zoom, middle
button or Shift + drag to pan, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo/redo, Ctrl+S save. In the test drive Esc returns to the editor.

## Site: Boca Chica (Starbase, Texas)

- **Build area:** a graded, fenced test field on the beach-ridge plain north-west of the launch site,
  **960 × 720 m = 48 × 36 tiles of 20 m**, completely dry (USGS 3DEP lidar: natural ground 0.6–1.8 m above sea
  level, ridges of ≈ 1 m every 30–60 m, lagoons and tidal flats around). The field is graded flat for the game; the
  real plain is not.
- **Outside the field** everything can be driven but not built on: the ridge plain, the tidal flats (soft, wet
  ground), the beach and dunes on the Gulf, Highway 4, and the launch site.
- **Launch site:** towers with catch arms, launch mounts, tank farm, bunkers, fence and roads
  at their real positions (OpenStreetMap), modelled without names or logos.
- **Data:** USGS 3DEP elevation and NAIP imagery (public domain), OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL).
- The build area is stored once, in the site's `meta.json` (`buildArea`), and read by the editor, the renderer and the
  physics.

## Grid and pieces

- Every piece occupies whole tiles (1 × 1, 1 × 2, 2 × 2 …) and is rotated in 90° steps, so road ends always meet
  flush. Decoration (cones, light masts, barriers) can be placed freely.
- Pieces are generated from profiles like the McGregor ramps (`src/shared/track.ts`): the same triangles are the
  physics collider (static trimesh) and the rendered mesh.
- Classic pieces: straight, curve, banked curve, slalom, chicane, ramp, jump with gap, bridge and elevated road on
  pillars, loop, corkscrew, tube, half pipe.
- Pieces for this car: wall ramp (as in the stream), ceiling track (fan suction, upside down), vertical wall, rings to
  fly through with the thrusters, landing platforms in the air, compressed-air station (refills the tank), boost pad.
- Obstacles and decoration: barrels, crates and container walls that break apart with the damage model, the vehicles
  of the stream, light masts, LED barriers, cones, grandstand.

## Game

- **Time trial as in the original:** the clock starts with the first metre; every piece of the course is a section to
  pass in order; skipping sections costs 5 s each, time spent off the track on the ground counts as penalty; the
  best time per layout is kept in the browser. *Planned:* replay, a ghost of the best run, a computer opponent.
- Without start/finish the level is free driving.
- A level can carry suggested realism settings (damage, compressor), which the player may accept or ignore
  (*planned*).

## Starship launch

- Super Heavy + Starship (≈ 123 m) on mount A: countdown, water deluge (T−10 s), Raptor ignition (T−3 s), 33 engines,
  the steam and sand cloud rolling out from the mount, liftoff and ascent over the Gulf, Max-Q, engine cut-off, hot
  staging. The booster flies back (boostback burn, fall, landing burn with 13 then 3 engines) and is caught by the
  tower A arms at T+6:58; the ship reaches orbit and, after one revolution in time lapse, comes back from the west
  (re-entry, belly flop, landing flip and burn) to be caught by the tower B arms at T+1:38:45, as announced for the
  next flights. Scripted after the public flight profiles (SpaceX Flight 5 timeline and telemetry; sources in
  `src/shared/starshipFlight.ts`), not simulated: Hermite tracks with position and velocity at every key.
- **Time lapse:** real time until the booster is caught and the ship is in orbit (T+9:00), then ×80 for the orbit, ×10
  for the entry, ×4 for the belly flop and real time again for the last 85 s: the whole show takes ≈ 13½ min of play.
- **Trigger:** a track piece "launch clearance" (countdown set in the editor, 30–180 s, default 60 s). Without it, the
  launch is announced when the lap ends at the finish (60 s). One launch per run; restarting the level puts the stack
  back on the mount. Free driving at Boca Chica gets a launch only through a level (a launch clearance on a track
  without start/finish); the other sites are unchanged.
- **Before liftoff:** announcement, a large countdown, an arrow with the distance to the launch site, the hint to a
  viewpoint (green marker on Highway 4 south of the pad, ≈ 540 m) and a red exclusion ring (400 m) around mount A.
  Inside it the pressure wave (leaving the mount with the speed of sound) and the jet blast push the car through the
  physics, so the damage setting applies as for any other impact.
- **After liftoff:** the mission clock (with the time-lapse factor), stream-style ticker messages (Max-Q, engine
  cut-off, stage separation, boostback burn, landing burn, ship in orbit, re-entry, landing flip, catch attempt,
  catch), a flight display (altitude and speed of booster and ship, a side view of the trajectory over the Gulf and
  back to the pad, "catch in m:ss" in game time) and a marker on the returning stage (an arrow at the screen edge when
  it is off screen). 30 s before each catch an alert announces it. The race clock keeps running.
- **Look and cost:** additive Raptor plumes (merged plume plus one cone per engine near the camera, ballooning and
  fading with altitude, a glow that keeps a stage visible as a point at 100 km), a dedicated particle cloud lit by the
  sun and the sky, catch arms and carriage with a damped bounce. Built when the launch is armed, so other worlds and
  the Boca Chica load are unaffected; on "low" and WebGL2 the cloud has fewer particles and the engine cones are left
  out. The stage nodes come straight from `starship_stack_lod1.glb` (`SuperHeavy`, `Starship`), the arms from the
  tower GLBs (`Carriage`, `Arm_L`, `Arm_R`, `CatchPoint`); no re-export was needed.
- **Test hooks:** `window.__rft.launchWarp(seconds)` moves the launch clock on, `launchWarp(0, met)` jumps to a
  mission time; `tests/perf/_launchshot.mjs` and `_launchclose.mjs` take screenshots of the whole flight,
  `tests/e2e/launch.spec.ts` checks it end to end.

## Levels

- Saved automatically in the browser (list of levels).
- Download as `.json` and load again (file dialog or drag and drop).
- Share link: the whole level, compressed, in the part of the URL after `#` (no backend).
- Schema with a version number; older versions are migrated on load.

## Assets

Visual assets (Starbase structures, Super Heavy and Starship, decoration and obstacle models, the editor's pixel
icons and entry screen) are generated by Codex (model GPT-6-Astra) as reproducible scripts (Blender headless,
`tools/worlds/blender/`, `tools/editor/`) in its own worktree, and reviewed here before they are merged: renders from
fixed views, no names or logos, node names as specified, size budget, `npm run compress-assets`.

## Look

- The editor and its entry screen look like a game from 1990: VGA-like palette, chunky pixel lettering, dialog boxes
  with bevelled DOS borders, a palette of small pixel icons. All of it is drawn for this project; no graphics, sounds,
  fonts or names of other games are used.
